Threats, Risks and Vulnerabilities

Overview

Threats, risks and vulnerabilities are fundamental concepts in cybersecurity and are often used together when assessing an organisation's security posture.

Understanding the relationship between these concepts helps organisations identify weaknesses and implement appropriate security controls.

---

Vulnerability

A vulnerability is a weakness that could be exploited by an attacker.

Examples:

- Weak passwords
- Unpatched software
- Misconfigured systems
- Outdated operating systems

A vulnerability does not automatically result in a security incident, but it creates an opportunity for one to occur.

---

Threat

A threat is any circumstance, event or actor capable of exploiting a vulnerability and causing harm.

Examples:

- Cybercriminals
- Malware
- Insider threats
- Natural disasters
- Human error

Threats have the potential to impact the confidentiality, integrity or availability of systems and data.

---

Risk

Risk is the likelihood that a threat will successfully exploit a vulnerability and the resulting impact on the organisation.

Examples:

- An attacker exploiting an unpatched server
- A phishing email leading to credential theft
- Ransomware encrypting critical business data

Risk assessments help organisations prioritise which vulnerabilities should be addressed first.

---

Example Scenario

Threat:
A cybercriminal attempts to gain access to company systems.

Vulnerability:
Employees use weak passwords.

Risk:
The attacker successfully guesses a password and gains unauthorised access to sensitive information.

---

Key Takeaways

- Vulnerabilities are weaknesses.
- Threats are events or actors that can exploit weaknesses.
- Risks represent the potential impact of successful exploitation.
- Organisations reduce risk by implementing security controls.
